# Playtest: min-maxer (2026-10-19)

Tester: source-unread agent (Claude Sonnet), agenda: find the optimal strategy and break the system. Build 4fc01c6 (HF 2.8.0), 360×780. Report verbatim below.

---

Report from the min-maxer.

I played about three and a half weeks of game time in total, in four runs. Runs were silent Monday, hold-the-first-option, hold-the-second-option, plain tap, and hold-first Monday to Saturday. I used a scripted phone browser at 360×780 and DPR 3, with real touch taps and 0.9 s presses. I did not read any code. Because of that, my choices were scripted, so I didn't read each scene's text before choosing. Some of the findings below are inferences from patterns across runs, not things the game told me.

**1. First minute**

I understood it quickly. Dilan has two voices in her head. I am one of them and the pencil voice is the other, and I pick lines from a short list. The two hint words ("later, maybe", "she might", "not today", "if you push") told me how likely she was to go along. I took them as odds.

I felt curious at first. About ten minutes later I started to feel I was handling a machine.

**2. Moments that worked**

- Holding an option gives a red "you insisted" tag and a grudging reply: "Fine. Since you insist." I liked it.
- The margin line "She kept that one" turned my held lines into her own habits ("Feet on the floor", "It's ten minutes", "Just say it plainly"). This is the best idea in the game.
- On the first Wednesday I said nothing all day. The entry read "I did that one myself. 'Feet on the floor.'" She also told Mum "not really" unprompted. I felt the pay-off from earlier pushing, and that was a good moment.
- The Saturday recap is a good ending. It shows the week as a grid, the voice note to Mum, and an "Another week" button.

**3. Moments that failed or confused me**

- **Option order.** The first option is not always my voice, and "the good one" is not always first. Coffee, laugh along and one more video are the pencil's lines. The game never says which line belongs to whom. That is fine for a normal player, but it means a min-maxer has to work out the pattern.
- **Scene timeout.** Scenes expire on a real-time clock and resolve to the pencil's choice ("You said nothing"). I lost scenes while thinking. A phone player will lose them too, and the cost is invisible.
- **Hold is a free override.** I held 40-plus times and it never failed. A hold on the second option (the lazy line) also always worked. The only cost I could see was an end-of-day line, "You pushed me a lot today. I did it. I didn't like it." I never saw that line change what she did.
- **Hold behaves slightly oddly.** "Laugh along" and the coffee held through to "The pencil had its way", which matches the pencil's own pick. A held line sometimes also gets a "She kept that one" tag and sometimes does not, and I could not tell why.
- **Plain taps often fail.** On Thursday, run 1, I tapped the first option on every scene. Almost every one ended in "After I sleep five more minutes, then I will" and "The pencil had its way". The day closed with "Thursday nearly got me." A tap that fails looks the same as a tap that nearly succeeded.
- **Small mismatches.** The clock showed 08:26 while the 08:29 entry was already written. The hint words shifted on the same scene between runs ("later, maybe" became "if you push" once Feet on the floor was kept).
- **Small mismatch in the screen text.** After a day ends there is a "tomorrow" heading in the notebook, so a script that taps the text "Tomorrow" hits the wrong thing. A real player would not hit this.

**4. Did my choices matter? Could the game be solved?**

Yes, it can be solved, and only partly by my choices.

- **Silence is the pencil's day.** Silent Monday gave oversleeping, "the ring road was solid", laughing along, the third coffee and scrolling past her stop. She does not do these things from malice. It is simply the default.
- **Silence after training is a strong strategy.** Run 1: I held Monday and Tuesday on a few lines, then went silent on Wednesday. Wednesday gave a clean day with three ticks, an "I did that one myself" trio and no pushback. That is nearly free.
- **Plain tapping is the weak one.** Thursday in run 1 was tap-only and failed. Silence was better than tapping, and holding was better than silence. I cannot say whether the Thursday failure came from tapping or from a bad Wednesday night, since I changed two things between those days.
- **Always-hold is dominant in the short run.** Run 3 was hold-first from Tuesday to Saturday. Every day showed the same ticks (alarm and call to Mum) and the same end line about pushing. Outcomes did not diverge, and I never felt a fork.
- **The best plan is "install, then idle".** Hold a small set of lines early (Feet on the floor, It's ten minutes, Just say it plainly) so they enter the margin as her own, then stay silent and let her act on them. This costs almost no attention.
- **The coffee loop is real, and harmless to my score.** Every Monday the pencil gets the coffee, and every night she lies awake past midnight ("The third coffee."). Even so, I held through the next morning each time. If I want to be cruel to myself I can hold the coffee forever. I cannot see a penalty.
- **Solving it is not fun.** The first two days are a delight. After that I knew the optimal macro: hold two or three scenes, then tick the rest and go silent. By Wednesday I was waiting for scripted scenes to finish, not choosing. The Saturday recap felt earned only when I had not min-maxed, and then only a little.

**5. Preachy, moralising or religious**

Nothing religious. A little moralising: "You pushed me a lot today. I did it. I didn't like it." reads as the game scolding the player for the very mechanic it gave me, and it does not change anything. Everything else is gentle.

**7. Verdict**

A lovely small idea, with a lovely moment in the habit margin. The game can be solved: hold to install, then stay silent. Solving it empties the middle of the week. Score: 6/10.

**8. The three changes I'd make first**

1. **Give hold a real, visible cost.** Make a held line consume something she feels (a shorter fuse, a later pushback, or a refusal she remembers). Then "always hold" stops being the answer, and "you pushed me a lot today" does some work.
2. **Make silence pay differently each day.** Right now a trained habit is a free coast. Let habits fade with disuse, or let some days hit a habit under stress, so silence needs maintenance.
3. **Let a tap sometimes land and show why it failed.** Plain taps almost always lose to the pencil. Either make a tap succeed some of the time, or add a short line saying why this one did not ("too tired", "she's still sore about Monday"). That would make the hint words ("later, maybe", "if you push") trustworthy odds instead of decoration.
