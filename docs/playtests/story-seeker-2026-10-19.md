I played week 1921145344 (game 0.1.0, HF 2.8.0) in full, from Monday 06:40 to the Saturday voice note. It was a 360×780 viewport at DPR 3 with touch, and I mixed taps, long-press insists and saying nothing. ## 1. First minute
The cover gave me one hook and it worked: "The other got there first." The alarm scene taught the visual grammar in one screen. Narration is in serif, the other voice is in pencil, and my options carry likelihood tags ("she might", "later, maybe"). I understood that I was a second voice and not a controller. I was curious within the first minute. I was not confused.

## 2. Moments that worked
- **The kept phrases.** "'Feet on the floor.' She kept that one." On Wednesday I said nothing, and she got up anyway with the pencil-styled line "Feet on the floor." That was the best moment of the week. The end screen's "what she did unasked" paid it off.
- **Running jokes.** The van impression went three ways across the week:
  - Monday, offscreen: she laughed at it.
  - Wednesday: I insisted on "Leave her alone", and the game said "Nobody did the impression again that week."
  - Thursday: Ece did an impression of Murat on the phone to his mother.
  That is a real arc built from three lines.
- **Small character details.** "Ece did a small fist pump she thought nobody saw." "Ece bowls like she drives. Murat keeps score in pen." "Mum held my hand under the table like I was nine."
- **The voice note.** It reflected my actual week: she didn't ring every day, she started the shelf, she admitted the routing mistake. "Mum replied with a voice note eleven minutes long" lands quietly and well. It is the right size of ending.
- **The pencil isn't a villain.** On Friday it pushed her toward bowling ("Be someone who goes to things"), and her own voice resisted. That made it read as a habit voice, not an evil one.
- **The notebook lines in her voice.** "I said it was me, and nothing fell down." "You pushed me a lot today. I did it. I didn't like it." These are the strongest writing in the game.

## What I think happened before the game, and what I inferred it from
**My reading:** she moved back to her home town in March, after something ended elsewhere. It might have been a relationship or a life she had been living. She is in a low, depressive stretch.
- "The town she came back to" on the cover.
- The beach photo "still" on the lock screen, mentioned every night. Something she hasn't let go of.
- Things dated to March: the notebook "she bought in March" and the shelf screws "in their little bag since March". She started setting up a new life and stalled.
- Signs of not coping:
  - Mum asking "if I was eating".
  - "Not really" when someone asked how she was at Aylin's dinner, and Mum putting the knife down to sit with her.
  - A gym plan she pays for and doesn't use.
  - Scrolling past her stop.
  - Fear of admitting fault: "nothing fell down" suggests that in the past, admitting fault cost her.

The family is warm and clearly worried: Mum, and her sister Aylin. I liked that the game never says any of this outright. **But it is thin.** Every clue is a single repeated line, and I am confident in "came back in March, low" and nothing deeper. I would have kept reading if the beach photo or March had paid off even once, for example as a Saturday line or a notebook memory. As it is, the beach photo is a hook with no catch.

## Do the people feel real?
- **Ece:** yes, the most real. She is the new driver, crying in the van, a son who plays football, the fist pump, bowling like she drives. She has an arc.
- **Murat:** half real. He sighs and fixes the mistake with her, says "fair", and notices her asleep. "He covered your route on Monday" hints at a kindness I never saw.
- **Mum:** real through small domestic detail: the neighbour's roof, "who won", the eleven-minute reply.
- **Aylin:** a name and a birthday. She "pretended not to listen" is the only thing she does.

## 3. Moments that failed or confused me
- **Key emotional beats happen offscreen as one log line.** Monday lunch ("She laughed. The impression got a second act.") explained Monday night's "Ece's face when she came back in". But I had to reconstruct that two days later. Wednesday's birthday dinner is the emotional centre of the week, and I got no scene at all. "Not really" appeared in the log after I tapped on with the day. As a story player I wanted to be in that room.
- **"She left it on read"** (Monday 07:31) and **"She left it. The kettle boiled twice."** (Wednesday 07:35). Left what? A message from whom? I never found out. On Wednesday it was probably Aylin's birthday message, but that is a guess.
- **The thirst lines read like a different story.** On Wednesday her own voice said "I can do it, but I'll be too thirsty for Aylin's dinner." On Friday, when I insisted on the gym: "I have to drink first." With "are you eating" and the lock-screen photo, I briefly read her as having a drinking problem. Nothing else supported that, so the lines read as glitches. If they mean fasting or simple dehydration, the game needs to say so somewhere, or cut the lines.
- **A dropped promise.** On Thursday she told Ece "After six, then", and Ece fist-pumped. At 18:11 she was on the bus home. The shadowing never happened, and the notebook didn't count it as kept or broken. Ece is the best character, so dropping her thread hurt.
- **The same three scenes every day.** The 15:20 coffee scene appeared word for word on Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday. The bus/Mum scene and the bed scene repeat too. The repetition is thematic, but by Thursday I was skimming the coffee scene. A varied line on the second occurrence would fix it ("The board again. The same red.").
- **The log contradicts itself.** "The pencil had its way" shows even when she did what I insisted on. An example is Friday's bowling, where the pencil agreed with me. Also, "Talk her through it" resolved as "She sent the pin". That was a deferral, but it is labelled as if I had lost outright.
- **Unclear end screen.** "two hands, Monday and Saturday: the other one → the other one / you → you". I don't know what it is telling me. Nothing changed, so it read as an empty diagram.
- **Missing scenes.** Friday night's bed scene was skipped, and the Saturday story has no shelf or Aylin follow-up even though "Ask Aylin to help on Saturday" was offered on Thursday.

## 4. Did choices matter? Difficulty?
Yes, in the right way for this genre:
- My kept phrases changed her later odds ("later, maybe" became "she might").
- The coffee cost her sleep the next night.
- Insisting worked but cost goodwill ("I didn't like it").

I understood what I was doing by Tuesday. The game is not hard and doesn't need to be. The one thing I didn't know was whether waiting counts as a choice. When I waited, the game moved on without me, and that is how I lost scenes I wanted.

## 5. Preachy, moralising or religious?
Not preachy. The pencil's lines are human ("You'll call her when you're human again"). The only lines that might be religious are the thirst ones, and they read as strange rather than religious. If they are a fasting cue, it is too hidden to register as one.

## 7. Verdict
This is a gentle, well-written week with one beautiful mechanic: her taking my words as her own. The people around her are sketched just enough to feel real. The back story is all teasers with no payoff, and the biggest emotional scenes happen while I'm not looking. **7/10.** I would play a second week, mainly to find out about March and the beach photo.

## 8. The three changes I'd make first
1. **Let me be present for the big beats.** Turn Monday lunch and Wednesday's dinner into actual scenes, or at least a held moment I can read, rather than a single log line I pass on the way.
2. **Pay off one back-story thread by Saturday.** The beach photo, March or "left it on read" would do. Give one line in the notebook or the voice note that confirms or deepens it. Also fix or explain the thirst lines.
3. **Close the threads you open, and vary the repeats.** Ece's "after six" should happen or be counted as broken, and so should the Aylin shelf offer. Give the repeating coffee, bus and bed scenes a second-occurrence variant.
