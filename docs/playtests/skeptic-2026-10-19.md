# Playtest: skeptic who hates moralising (2026-10-19)

Tester: source-unread agent (Claude Sonnet), agenda: hunt for preachiness and a hidden correct answer; test whether siding with the bad voice is ever right. Build 4fc01c6 (HF 2.8.0), 360×780. Report verbatim below.

---

**Report from the skeptic playthrough of The Two Voices**

I played Monday morning to Thursday evening. That is three full days plus the fourth afternoon, and every bed page up to Wednesday night. I screenshotted at 360×780, DPR 3, touch. Screenshots are in `/private/tmp/claude-504/-Users-abdul-code-human-framework/675a9839-8299-4863-91be-017a92a76ff0/scratchpad/pt-skeptic/` (`s1.png` to `s31.png`).

**1. First minute**
- I understood it right away. "There are two voices in her head. One is yours. The other got there first." is a strong, clean hook.
- I braced for a sermon and it didn't arrive. There is no tutorial lecture, only "Tap one to say it. Or say nothing."
- The first choice, "Up. Feet on the floor." against "Five more.", is a morality-play setup. It didn't read as one because the pencil voice sounds like me: "The alarm is more of a suggestion."

**2. Moments that worked**
- "Mum gave her the biggest slice anyway." I insisted on a white lie ("Yes, Mum.") and was warmly rewarded. No judgment, just cake. This is what I came to check, and it passed.
- The third coffee (Tue 15:31) works. "The board was green by five." The bad voice wins now and pays later, because Wednesday starts "She lay awake past midnight." Delayed cost without a lecture.
- "Aylin is thirty. That can't be right." Small and human.
- "Murat looked at Ece's scanner for a long time." The consequence of my lie lands on a real person, and the game never says so out loud.
- "Press and hold an option to insist" and the red "you insisted" tag. A mechanic with no moral label.
- "Feet on the floor. She kept that one." Habits form through a phrase she keeps.

**3. Moments that failed or confused**
- Saying nothing is undiscoverable. I found it by accident: waiting about 30 seconds, or tapping "on with the day". Nothing tells you that silence is a move. Bedtime made it worse: Monday and Wednesday auto-resolved to "It was not one more video", with no input from me at all.
- **The hint labels** ("she might", "later, maybe", "if you push", "not today") look like probabilities, and they sometimes misled me:
  - I tapped "Eat outside" ("not today") and it worked fine.
  - I tapped "Laugh along" ("she might") and she refused it.
  - On Monday "Laugh along" never got tested, because I took "Eat outside" instead.
- The line "I listened to you, and I'm not sure I should have." was on every bed page. That includes Tuesday and Wednesday, after I had insisted on getting up and had her call Mum. It reads as a stale verdict on me, and it is the closest the game comes to judging the player. If it is meant to change with my choices, I couldn't see it change.
- The same night line, "It was not one more video. The pencil had its way.", appeared three nights running.
- "The pencil had its way" is a cryptic label. Is the pencil the bad voice? I worked it out, but it took a while.

**4. Did my choices matter? Was it too easy? Did I know what I was doing?**
- Choices mattered mostly in their downstream notes: the Ece blame, the "Murat knows it wasn't the ring road" bed note, and the carried-over tomorrow list.
- **The hidden correct answer.** Social cruelty and dishonesty toward others are quietly overridden. The self-indulgent voice wins freely (sleeping in, coffee, scrolling). Four cases:
  - Wed lunch: I tapped "Laugh along" and she said "Not that — I'll eat outside." (`s25.png`)
  - Thu lunch: the same thing happened again.
  - Thu 08:25: I tapped the ring-road lie and she said "After I own up, then I will." The honest answer cost nothing: "Happens. Board's yours."
  - Thu Ece moment: this one went my way. "Let it go" won, with a visible cost (Ece apologised twice).
- So the real asymmetry is this. The bad voice wins at being lazy, and loses at being unkind or dishonest.
  - Lying on Tuesday went through, then stung later.
  - Lying on Thursday got overridden, and the honesty cost nothing, which is the preachy outcome. Honesty being free there is the weakest moment in the game.
  - The white lie to Mum was the only clear case where the "bad" voice was right and rewarded.
- Difficulty: nothing felt hard or scored. It felt gentle and observational. I never knew whether I was "winning", which is mostly good.

**5. Preachy, moralising, religious**
- Nothing religious. I saw no prayer, fasting or scripture.
- Preachiness is low, and it never says "should". The one exception is Dilan's own line "not sure I should have." Keep it as her voice, not mine.
- The ledger on the bed page, with items struck through and a "Tomorrow" list, is a quiet guilt device. It is only a mild one, because it is framed as her thoughts.

**6. Bugs and layout at 360×780**
- Large empty band between the header and the log on a fresh scene (`s2.png`, `s8.png`). Content sits top and bottom with a dead middle, and the stray rule near the vertical middle looks accidental.
- The header menu button jumps position between screens: y≈24 on the game screen versus y≈34 on bed and menu screens. Its x also shifts from 322 to 344 to 331 between nights. I measured this in tap coordinates; it is a small jitter.
- Header clock and log timestamps disagree. The header showed 06:44 while the log's latest entry was 07:11 (`s8.png`). This could be intentional, with the clock showing the moment of decision and the log showing outcomes. It looked like a bug to me.
- Reloading the page resumes at the last decision point, not at the idle clock. That is fine, but the cover only offers "Continue the week", "New week" and "Replay".

**7. Verdict:** A gentle, well-written week that mostly avoids the sermon. It falls into a soft preference for honesty and kindness at exactly the moments where being bad would be most interesting. **7/10.**

**8. First three changes**
1. Let the bad voice win a social or honesty moment and make it pay, at least once, in the same way the coffee does. The Thursday ring-road override and the "Laugh along" refusals should sometimes just happen. Otherwise a skeptic is right that the angel is always right when it counts.
2. Make "say nothing" visible: a labelled hold-or-wait action, not a side effect of waiting. Make the hints honest too, so "she might" doesn't turn into a refusal.
3. Fix the stale "I listened to you, and I'm not sure I should have." line. Either tie it to my actual choices or drop it, and vary the repeated nightly line "It was not one more video."
